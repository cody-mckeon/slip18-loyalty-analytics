Why this is useful

Different groups may respond differently to incentive amounts. The policy chooses the offer with the highest observed training profit within each tier, then tests the fixed choice on fresh customers.

An average tier's spending is not the same as the *incremental* value of offering it an incentive. Segment choice here is learned from randomized offer comparisons.

How this study was designed

01 · Start with the business decision

The fictional marketing director and finance partner defined a $10 minimum meaningful incremental contribution per assigned customer, based on a 100,000-member audience, hypothetical fixed rollout expense and required net upside.

02 · Plan sample size before outcomes

An independent simulated 20,000-member preperiod estimated customer-profit variability. The test planned for 80% power and a two-sided 5% significance level. Its design file was written before outcomes within the reproducible script, not registered externally.

03 · Protect the comparison

Assign people randomly to offer or control. Count everyone assigned, including nonvisitors. Net contribution subtracts modeled visit expenses, actual incentive redemption, and contact costs; fixed rollout costs are applied only to the rollout scenario.

04 · Learn offers, then freeze the policy

The separate optimization study randomized 160,000 simulated customers across four incentive levels for training. It evaluated the locked tier policy on 90,000 fresh randomized customers across three strategies.

05 · Distinguish evidence from decisions

Statistical significance does not guarantee a financially meaningful effect. Confidence intervals show uncertainty around average differences; neither study establishes real-world profitability.

Risks, limitations & next decisions

Before any real rollout

  • Verify actual historical profit distribution, eligibility, email permission and missing outcome rates.
  • Obtain Finance sign-off for minimum worthwhile lift, full campaign economics and fixed costs.
  • Validate random assignment, sample ratios, offer redemptions, overlap and operational constraints.
  • Prospectively power a follow-up policy comparison and define primary endpoints and interim-look rules.
  • Replicate personalized-offer gains, assess multiple comparisons and conduct longer-window retention checks.
